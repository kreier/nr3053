Why Stock MTD Numbers Shift by One

On the stock Viettel firmware, mtd0 exposes the entire raw 256 MiB chip ("spi0.0"), so individual partitions start at mtd1. Under ImmortalWrt, BL2 is mtd0. The physical NAND offsets of the first 6 MiB are identical:

Physical NAND Offset Stock Viettel ROM ImmortalWrt 25.12 Migration Note
0x000000–0x100000BL2 (mtd1)BL2 (mtd0)Keep stock BL2 unless specifically needed (high brick risk).
0x100000–0x200000u-boot-env (mtd2)u-boot-env (mtd1)Legacy env; ImmortalWrt stores active env inside UBI.
0x200000–0x400000Factory (mtd3)Factory (mtd2)NEVER ERASE — holds Wi-Fi RF EEPROM & MACs.
0x400000–0x600000FIP (mtd4)FIP (mtd3)Flash ImmortalWrt U-Boot FIP here (same offset 0x400000).
0x600000–0x0f000000firmware (32M) + firmware2 (32M) + rootfs_data (142M)ubi (234 MiB Native UBI)Incompatible OS layout — cannot run sysupgrade directly from stock.
Native UBI vs. Legacy NMBM Warning
Stock Viettel firmware uses MediaTek's NMBM bad-block mapping layer and flat firmware/firmware2 partitions, whereas ImmortalWrt 25.12 uses Native UBI (without NMBM) across 234 MiB. Once ImmortalWrt U-Boot formats the UBI container, stock OS data above 0x600000 is wiped. Always back up stock MTD partitions (especially Factory) first.
